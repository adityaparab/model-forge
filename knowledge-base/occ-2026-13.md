# OCC Bulletin 2026-13: Revised Guidance on Model Risk Management

## What the bulletin is

OCC Bulletin 2026-13 is the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency's issuance of the interagency Revised Guidance on Model Risk Management. The same guidance was issued by the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System as SR letter 26-2 on April 17, 2026. The transmittal letter of SR 26-2 names three agencies as issuers: the Federal Reserve Board, the OCC and the FDIC. Each agency transmits the shared guidance through its own channel, and for national banks and federal savings associations supervised by the OCC that channel is the OCC bulletin series.

Because the guidance is a single interagency document, this corpus does not restate it here. The full text of the guidance, including its scope, definitions and footnotes, is in the `sr26-2` document. Readers who need the OCC's own wording about which earlier OCC issuances it replaces, or which institutions it applies to, should consult the bulletin page named in the front-matter.

## Relationship to SR 26-2 and SR 11-7

The 2011 model risk guidance was itself an interagency product: the Federal Reserve issued it as SR 11-7 and the OCC issued the same text as OCC Bulletin 2011-12. The 2026 revision follows the same pattern, with the FDIC now also named as an issuing agency. SR 26-2 states that it supersedes SR 11-7 and SR 21-8 (the 2021 interagency statement on model risk management for systems supporting Bank Secrecy Act and anti-money laundering compliance).

When this corpus refers to the substance of the revised guidance, it cites SR 26-2. When a document needs to note that the OCC issued the same guidance, it can cite this bulletin as the parallel issuance.

## Points of substance, by reference to SR 26-2

The following points come from the SR 26-2 text in this corpus and apply equally to the OCC issuance because the guidance is shared:

- The guidance emphasises a risk-based approach to model risk management, tailored to an institution's model risk profile and to the size and complexity of its operations.
- It is expected to be most relevant to banking organisations with more than $30 billion in total assets, while noting it may also be relevant to smaller organisations with significant model risk exposure.
- It defines a model as a complex quantitative method, system or approach that applies statistical, economic or financial theories to process input data into quantitative estimates, and excludes simple arithmetic and deterministic rule-based processes.
- It states that the guidance does not set enforceable standards or prescriptive requirements.
- It places generative AI and agentic AI models outside its scope, describing them as novel and rapidly evolving, while stating that an organisation's broader risk management and governance practices should still guide the controls applied to tools and systems the guidance does not cover. The principles of the guidance apply to traditional statistical and quantitative models and to non-generative, non-agentic AI models.

## Relevance to a PD scorecard

A probability-of-default scorecard built from weight-of-evidence binning and logistic regression is a traditional statistical model and sits within the scope described above. The guidance's themes of sound development, validation, ongoing monitoring, governance and documentation therefore bear directly on how such a model is built and reviewed. This summary describes the guidance only; it makes no statement about whether any particular model or system meets it.
